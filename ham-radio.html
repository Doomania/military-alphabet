Ham Radio Reference

Q-codes · Abbreviations · Band plan · RST · NZ callsigns

Q-codes are three-letter shorthand (QTH = my location, QSL = I confirm receipt) that let amateur radio operators exchange standard information regardless of language. This page covers the most-used Q-codes plus abbreviations, the HF band plan and RST signal reports.

Essentials

NZ prefix ZL: ZL1=Auckland/Northland · ZL2=Wellington · ZL3=Christchurch · ZL4=Dunedin

73 = Best regards  ·  88 = Love and kisses  ·  CQ = Calling any station  ·  SK = End of contact

Frequently Asked Questions

What does QRZ mean?

QRZ asks 'who is calling me?'. It is one of the three-letter Q-codes standardized so operators can exchange routine information regardless of language — QTH is 'my location', QSL is 'I confirm receipt'.

What does 73 mean in ham radio?

'73' means 'best regards' — a sign-off inherited from the 1859 Western Union number code list. '88' means 'love and kisses', so use 73 unless you know the operator very well.

Do I need a license to listen to ham radio?

No — listening (shortwave listening, SWL) is legal in most countries with any receiver. Transmitting requires an amateur licence; in New Zealand that is the General Amateur Operator Certificate administered by RSM.

What is an RST report?

RST scores a signal by Readability 1–5, Strength 1–9 and Tone 1–9 (tone applies to Morse/CW only). '599' — perfectly readable, extremely strong, pure tone — is the classic contest report.
