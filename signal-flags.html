Signal Flags

ICS nautical flags A-Z · Visual designs · Maritime meanings · NATO codewords

The International Code of Signals assigns each letter A–Z a distinct maritime flag with its own single-flag meaning — Alfa for 'diver down', Bravo for 'dangerous cargo'. Ships still fly them for legal signalling and naval ceremony.

How to Use

Single flag signals have specific meanings at sea — hoisting Flag A means I have a diver down, keep clear.

NC hoisted together (November + Charlie) = International distress signal.
Oscar alone = Man overboard — the most urgent single-flag signal at sea.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Alfa (A) flag mean?

The white-and-blue swallow-tailed Alfa flag means 'I have a diver down; keep well clear at slow speed'. Flying it is a legal requirement for dive boats in many jurisdictions.

What does the Bravo (B) flag mean?

The solid red swallow-tailed Bravo flag means 'I am taking in, discharging or carrying dangerous goods' — historically gunpowder, today fuel, ammunition and explosives.

How do ships signal distress with flags?

Hoisting November over Charlie (NC) is the International Code of Signals distress signal. The Victor flag alone means 'I require assistance' — serious, but short of distress.
